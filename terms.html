Terms and privacy · plain words

What Mornward records, and what it never does.

Mornward is in free beta. To learn whether it actually helps, it counts a few anonymous things about each routine. It never collects anything you write, and there's no account to sign up for.

Who's behind it

Mornward is made by one independent developer based in Thailand, who decides how this data is used (the "data controller" under Thailand's Personal Data Protection Act, PDPA). For questions or any request about your data, email mornward@proton.me.

✓ What Mornward keeps track of

  • A random device ID made on your phone, like 3f2a9c…. It isn't linked to your name, email or any account, but it does link your records to each other, so they're pseudonymous rather than fully anonymous. Each record also gets its own random ID, so a record sent twice is stored once.
  • The date, and the time the record arrived.
  • Which steps of the routine happened: you started it, finished or skipped the breathing (and how many breaths you'd done if you skipped), saved or skipped mini tasks (how many, whether you kept the two suggested ones or changed them, and whether you wrote your own), reached the end, and how many seconds it took. Never what any task says.
  • Whether you opened Mornward from your home screen, or from a calendar reminder or an alarm shortcut, and on Android whether you said yes to adding it there.
  • Whether you tapped to set up a morning reminder: opened the Google Calendar link, made a calendar file, or copied the alarm-shortcut link. Not whether you saved it or whether it went off; Mornward can't see that.
  • Which link you came from (like "reddit"), if the link says so. Sent once, with your first routine.

Earlier beta versions (until October 2026) also counted setup answers, morning steps, ratings, which listed app you opened and which first-visit screens you reached. Those records are kept and deleted on the same terms.

🔒 What stays private and personal

  • Your name, email, phone number or any account. There's no sign-up.
  • Your mini tasks, or anything else you write. Not a single word, only whether you saved one.
  • Your earlier tasks or notes. They stay in this browser on your phone.
  • Your location, contacts, photos or other apps. Mornward has no access to them.
  • Anything at all once you tap Turn off.

Beta terms

  • Free beta. Mornward is free while in beta and is provided as it is. Features may change, and things may break.
  • Not medical advice. It's a habit tool, not medical or mental-health advice. If you're struggling, please talk to someone qualified.
  • Your writing lives on your phone. Your mini tasks (and notes from earlier versions) are saved only in your browser. Clearing your browser data deletes them, and they can't be recovered.
  • Calendar reminders. If you add one, it's made on your phone and saved in your own calendar, with a plain link to Mornward. The link carries no ID, settings or tasks (reminders made by versions before beta-5 included your random ID after "#"). Mornward never receives the event. If you choose Google Calendar, Google keeps it like any other event you create. Delete it in your calendar any time.
  • iPhone home-screen icon. On iPhone the icon keeps its own storage, separate from Safari. So that you aren't counted as a new person, the icon's start address holds your random ID, the terms version and your stats choice after "#", on your phone only. It never holds your tasks, and with stats off it holds no ID.
  • Age. You're at least 13 years old.
  • Agreeing. You agree by tapping Begin the first time, below a short note that links here. If these terms change, that note shows again and tapping Begin agrees to the new version. Agreeing is also your consent to the anonymous stats described here, including storing them in the United States. You can withdraw it any time by turning stats off.

Your controls

  • Turn stats off under Privacy and your data, on the screen you see after finishing a routine. Nothing is sent from your phone after that, and anything waiting to be sent is deleted.
  • See or delete your stats. Open Privacy and your data after a routine and email the anonymous ID shown there to mornward@proton.me. Every record with that ID is deleted, or sent to you if you asked to see it, within 30 days and usually much sooner. We don't collect your name, email or contact details, so without the ID we can't find your records. Anyone who has your ID could pick out your records, so share it only with us.
  • Delete everything on your phone with the button in the same place. Everything you write stays on your phone, in this browser's storage, so clearing your browser data deletes it too.
  • Complain. If you're unhappy with how your data is handled, please email first. You can also complain to Thailand's Personal Data Protection Committee (PDPC).

Where it goes

The anonymous records are stored in a Supabase database in the United States. The app can only add records; it can't read, change or delete anything, including other people's records. Like most websites, Supabase keeps short-term technical logs of requests, which can include an IP address and browser type. Those aren't used for the beta stats.

The United States may not protect personal data to the same standard as Thai law. The records hold no name, email or contact details, and only the developer can read them. They're used only to learn whether the beta helps, and they're never sold, shared or used for ads.

How long: the records are deleted within 90 days after the beta ends, or sooner if you ask.
